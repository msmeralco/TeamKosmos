# Sol Pitch Script (10 minutes)

## Slide 0: Title (0:00 to 0:15)

Good morning, everyone. We are Team Kosmos, and this is Sol, our clean energy partner for the IDOL Hackathon. Let us begin at three in the morning on a quiet Philippine street, where most houses are dark and a few windows are still lit.

## Slide 1: Hook (0:15 to 0:50)

In January 2026, Meralco's typical household rate was about ₱12.95 per kilowatt-hour. By October it had reached ₱15.06, which is 16 percent higher. For an illustrative home that uses 450 kilowatt-hours a month, the bill has risen from about ₱5,830 to about ₱6,780. When bills climb like this, many families start asking whether solar could help them.

## Slide 2: Compare (0:50 to 1:30)

Answering that question is difficult. Three sellers can offer setups that look identical on paper, and published installed prices run from ₱45 to ₱75 per watt. Industry research reports that buyers have no reliable way to tell a qualified installer from a cheap one. The DTI has also proposed mandatory certification of solar products because of substandard goods. A family that wants to choose well has very little to go on.

## Slide 3: Understand (1:30 to 2:20)

Even after choosing a seller, it is hard to predict how much you will save. Your panels power your home first. When they make more than you need, the extra power goes to the grid, and Meralco credits it at a lower rate than the rate you pay when you buy electricity. At night you buy power back at the full rate. If you have no REC meter, your generation is estimated by a formula. Sol exists to make each of these points clear and checkable.

## Slide 4: Meet Sol (2:20 to 3:20)

This is Sol, an AI agent for anything solar. Sol starts with your address. It drops a pin on the map, outlines your roof, and measures it. On this roof, eight panels fit, and you can see the crew placing them on the layout. Every number you will see from here on is based on that measured roof.

## Slide 5: Ask (3:20 to 4:30)

After the roof, we simply talk to Sol. The household says that its bill is ₱6,780 a month, that its budget is up to ₱300,000, and that it wants to cut the bill. Sol confirms that this is about 450 kilowatt-hours a month, and it asks one follow-up question about the goal. Then it tells the household what it will do next, which is to compare grid only, solar, and solar with a battery for this roof, as ranges.

## Slide 6: Compare options (4:30 to 5:40)

Most tools stop at one confident number. Sol shows a range. Over ten years, staying on the grid costs about ₱813,000. Solar lands between ₱248,000 and ₱457,000, and solar with a battery lands between ₱287,000 and ₱502,000. The spread comes from two things, the price you are quoted and the weather year you get, which is why the three houses sit under different skies. A battery gives you backup power, but at these prices it has not paid for itself within ten years.

## Slide 7: Sol picks (5:40 to 6:50)

Now Sol makes the choices. It opens a catalog of parts and selects the ones that fit the roof and the goal. For this home it picks the 550 watt panel, because eight of them fit, and the 5 kilowatt inverter, which suits 4.4 kilowatts of panels. It skips both batteries, since a battery would not pay for itself in ten years.

Next, Sol looks at the quotations from installers. The cheapest quote leaves the permit and the net-metering paperwork to the customer, and the most expensive quote is over the ₱300,000 budget. Sol picks the middle quote, which stays inside the budget and files the paperwork for the customer.

The parts selection is built on our optimizer. The installer match is our proposal for the future. The catalog and the quotes on this slide are samples, and no installer has agreed to take part.

## Slide 8: Assumptions and sources (6:50 to 7:40)

Every number in Sol needs a source. The weather layer is hourly PVGIS data, which is a modeled estimate. The tariff is Meralco's October 2026 rate of ₱15.06 per kilowatt-hour. The export credit is approximated by Meralco's generation charge, and the prices come from public listings that the customer should confirm with the seller.

Sol also searches a local library of Meralco and DOE documents using a method called retrieval-augmented generation, or RAG. It returns passages together with links to the original pages. The language model uses them for facts and citations, and tools do all of the calculations.

## Slide 9: Passport (7:40 to 8:40)

When a household goes solar, Sol gives it a Solar Passport, which is the household's proof that it made the switch. The installer confirms the installation, and the green seal on the Passport shows that confirmation. Once the installation is confirmed, the Passport unlocks vouchers and discounts. This is how Sol covers the rewards part of the challenge.

The rewards programme is our proposal. The vouchers on screen are samples, a partner would fund the real ones, and nothing has been agreed with Meralco or with any business.

## Slide 10: Who it helps (8:40 to 9:30)

Sol helps three groups. Customers get an honest range of outcomes and a Passport that earns vouchers and discounts for going solar. Business owners, meaning the installers and sellers, can show customers a clear layout and a quote that holds up, and they can fund rewards for customers who go solar. Meralco gets better-prepared customers, who can be pointed to accredited installers and to the right grid pathway.

The customer features are the core of what we are building. The installer and Meralco parts are our proposal, and nothing is agreed with either of them. Sol promises no Meralco benefit.

## Slide 13: Close (9:30 to 10:00)

Sol helps a household compare honestly, understand clearly, and go solar with a reward at the end. Thank you, and we are happy to take your questions.
