HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE
- Every answer is written to be said out loud in 15 to 30 seconds. The "if pushed" line is the second-level answer.
- Rule 1: never claim something that is not built. Use the STATUS TAGS below.
- Rule 2: when a question hits a weak spot, admit it in one sentence, then say what we did about it. Judges reward honesty and punish bluffing.
- Rule 3: pivot to the working demo whenever you can. "Let me show you" beats "we plan to".

STATUS TAGS (use these words exactly)
  [BUILT]    Works in the app today.
  [PARTIAL]  Code exists, but it is limited or uses sample data.
  [PITCH]    Our proposal. Nothing is agreed with any partner. Say so.
  [NOT BUILT] A next step. Do not imply otherwise.

THE ONE-BREATH PITCH (memorize this)
"Sol is an AI agent that helps a Meralco household go solar. You give it your
address, bill, budget and goal. It measures your roof on a satellite map,
simulates grid-only, solar and solar-plus-battery hour by hour over ten years,
picks parts and installers that fit, and issues a signed Solar Passport that
unlocks vouchers once an installer confirms the install. The AI never does the
math. Tools do. And every number it says is checked."

THE THREE-LINE ANSWER TO "WHY NOT JUST USE CLAUDE / CHATGPT / GEMINI?"
1. A chatbot talks. Sol measures your roof, runs a real simulation, and shows its sources.
2. A chatbot can make up numbers. In Sol the AI cannot calculate, and a guard blocks any number the tools did not produce.
3. A chatbot ends at advice. Sol ends at a signed Passport and a redeemable voucher.


==============================================================================
SECTION 1. "WHY NOT JUST USE CLAUDE / CHATGPT?" (EXPECT THIS FIRST)
==============================================================================

Q1.1  What is the difference between Sol and Claude (or ChatGPT, Gemini)?
A: Claude is a general assistant. Sol is a product built around an AI agent.
   Sol has a satellite roof outline, a validated solar simulation (pvlib with
   PVGIS-ERA5 weather), a budget- and roof-constrained optimizer, a parts
   catalog, a Meralco/DOE document library, a number guard, and a signed
   Passport with redeemable vouchers. Claude has none of those unless someone
   builds them around it. In fact, Sol uses a large language model (Gemini) as
   its conversation layer. The model is the voice. The product is everything
   around it.
   If pushed: asking "should I go solar?" in a chat gives a paragraph. Sol gives
   a roof outline, a 10-year table with a best case and a bad year, a package
   list, and a verifiable record. [BUILT]

Q1.2  Why can't I just paste my bill into Claude and ask?
A: You can, and you will get a plausible answer. Three problems:
   (a) It does not know your roof. Area, tilt and direction decide output, and
       Sol outlines the roof from OpenStreetMap on a satellite map.
   (b) It estimates solar output from memory. Sol runs an hourly simulation on
       real modeled weather for your location.
   (c) It can state wrong figures confidently. In Sol, numbers come only from
       tools, and the number guard rejects anything the tools did not return.
   If pushed: a chatbot has no way to know it is wrong. Sol is built so it
   cannot be wrong in the way chatbots are wrong. [BUILT]

Q1.3  Claude can read images and do math with code. Is that not enough?
A: A model with code execution can compute something if the user supplies the
   right inputs and knows what to ask. A household does not know to ask about
   self-consumption share, export credit below retail, transformer hosting
   limits or Net Metering fees. Sol builds those checks into the flow ("Before
   you invest": right size, right location, right provider, know the
   requirements) so nobody has to know what to ask. [BUILT]

Q1.4  Could Claude do everything Sol does if you gave it tools?
A: Largely yes. That is the point. Sol IS that: a model with tools. Our work is
   the tools, data, guard rails and the loop after the advice (Passport,
   vouchers). A generic assistant does not come with a Philippine tariff model,
   a Meralco rules library, a DOE installer registry, a parts catalog, or a
   signing key. We built the missing parts. Being honest: the model layer is
   replaceable, and that is a design choice, not a weakness.

Q1.5  What does Sol know that a general AI does not?
A: Local, sourced and current facts: Meralco tariff and Net Metering pages, the
   DOE circular DC2026-08-0017, the DOE Solar PV Installer Registry (77 firms
   in Metro Manila, Central Luzon and CALABARZON, shown as "registered, not
   endorsed"), 25 real installer packages with published prices, and
   PVGIS-ERA5 weather bundled for Metro Manila. It shows the source passage and
   link for each rules answer. [BUILT]

Q1.6  Is Sol just a wrapper around an LLM?
A: No. Of the backend, the LLM is one provider among two. The rule-based
   ScriptedProvider runs the whole flow with no LLM at all. The substance is
   deterministic services: load estimate, irradiance, hourly simulation,
   battery dispatch, net-metering billing, finance, optimizer, layout,
   readiness checks, Passport signing. A wrapper has nothing to run when the
   model is off. Sol does. [BUILT]

Q1.7  Why would a household use Sol instead of just asking an installer?
A: Installers quote to sell. Sol gives an independent range before the first
   call, shows how exports are really credited, and hands the household a
   checklist of what to ask. The household arrives informed, and the installer
   arrives with a layout and a checked brief. [BUILT: advice and checklist.
   PITCH: that installers fund rewards.]

Q1.8  Why not Google Project Sunroof, Aurora, or other solar calculators?
A: Be honest: we did not benchmark them feature by feature. Our differences:
   (1) Philippines-specific: Meralco tariff, Net Metering rules, DOE
       registry. 
   (2) Ranges (bad year, expected, best case) rather than one number.
   (3) Conversational agent that explains and answers rule questions with
       sources.
   (4) Passport and rewards: the journey continues after the estimate.
   We would not claim our roof modelling beats a company with aerial imagery
   and 3D models. Ours is an outline from OpenStreetMap or a hand-drawn polygon.

Q1.9  If models keep improving, will Sol be obsolete?
A: Better models make Sol's conversation better, and the provider is
   swappable. They do not give anyone a signing key, a verified installer
   registry, a redemption ledger, or funded vouchers. The moat is data, rules,
   and the trust loop, not the model.

SECTION 2. WHAT MAKES SOL UNIQUE AND INNOVATIVE
==============================================================================

Q2.1  What is the single most innovative thing?
A: The combination: an AI agent that cannot do arithmetic. The model
   orchestrates and explains, deterministic tools produce every number, and a
   guard checks the reply against tool output before you see it. Most AI
   advisors trust the model's math. Sol structurally cannot. [BUILT]

Q2.2  What is the second?
A: The Solar Passport. Advice normally ends at a PDF. Sol ends at a signed,
   shareable, QR-verifiable record (Ed25519 signature) that moves through three
   stages: plan, installed, redeemed. It is the bridge between "I got a quote"
   and "I got a reward". [BUILT; install confirmation flow is PARTIAL, see 6.x]

Q2.3  What else is new?
A: - Honest ranges, not a single number: bad year, expected, best case.
   - Export credit modelled at Meralco's blended generation charge, editable,
     with a lower-credit sensitivity, not the retail rate.
   - The optimizer chooses from a real catalog. The model cannot invent parts.
   - "Before you invest" checks on every option, built from Meralco's own four
     points.
   - Works without any AI key (rule-based fallback), so the demo cannot die
     from an API outage.
   - A data-honesty register (ASSUMPTIONS.yaml): every assumption has unit,
     source, date, and a verified flag. The agent says so when an unverified
     number drives a result. [BUILT]

Q2.4  Is it innovative or just integration?
A: Both, and we will not pretend otherwise. The individual ingredients (PVGIS,
   pvlib, OpenStreetMap, an LLM) are known. The novelty is the closed loop:
   roof -> simulation -> ranges -> parts -> installer -> signed Passport ->
   voucher, with an AI that is forbidden from inventing a figure. Integration
   done with discipline is the innovation here.

Q2.5  What is the "wow" moment in the demo?
A: Type an address. The roof is outlined. Say your bill and budget. Ten
   seconds later you see a three-way 10-year comparison with a bad year, and
   the battery is honestly shown as NOT paying back. Then the Passport opens
   like a booklet and a voucher redeems once.

Q2.6  Why does the battery honesty matter?
A: Most vendors upsell a battery. Sol shows that at current prices it does not
   pay for itself within ten years and picks it only if backup is your goal.
   Trust is the product. [BUILT]

Q2.7  Did you invent a new algorithm?
A: No. We used established methods (pvlib, hourly dispatch, BM25 retrieval).
   Our contribution is applying them to a Philippine household decision with a
   truthfulness guard and a reward loop.

SECTION 3. THE PROBLEM AND THE MARKET
==============================================================================

Q3.1  What problem are you solving?
A: Households want to go solar but cannot tell a qualified quote from a cheap
   one, do not know exports are credited below the retail rate, and see no
   reward for acting. Meralco's typical household rate rose 16% from January to
   October 2026 (₱12.95 to ₱15.06 per kWh), so the pressure is real.

Q3.2  How big is the pain?
A: Published installed prices run ₱45 to ₱75 per watt, a wide spread. A buyer
   without a reference cannot judge it. That spread is the gap Sol fills.

Q3.3  Who is the customer?
A: Primary: a Meralco household considering rooftop solar. Secondary:
   installers and sellers who want better-prepared leads. Tertiary: Meralco and
   regulators who want customers to enter the correct grid pathway.

Q3.4  Why Challenge 1: Clean Energy Adoption and Rewards?
A: Adoption: Sol lowers the confusion that stops people. Rewards: the Passport
   turns a confirmed install into vouchers and discounts. Both halves of the
   challenge are in one flow.

Q3.5  Why the Philippines specifically?
A: High tariffs, strong sun, Net Metering rules, and a registry of DOE
   installers. Also, existing tools are built for US/EU tariffs and rules.

Q3.6  What is your market size?
A: We have not sized a market and we will not invent a number on stage. The
   addressable group is Meralco residential customers. If asked for a figure,
   say: "We have not sized it. We focused on proving the product works first."

Q3.7  Who are your competitors?
A: Direct: installer websites with a basic calculator, global solar tools
   (Sunroof, Aurora, etc.), and generic chatbots. Indirect: doing nothing.
   Our edge is local rules, honest ranges, a verified-number AI, and a
   reward loop. We did not do a formal competitive audit; say so if asked.

SECTION 4. HOW IT WORKS (PRODUCT)
==============================================================================

Q4.1  Walk me through the flow.
A: 1. Roof: type an address, Sol outlines the roof from OpenStreetMap on a
      satellite map. Adjust it or draw it by hand.
   2. Chat: bill, budget, goal (cut the bill, best 10-year savings, fastest
      payback, blackout backup). If no goal is given, it defaults to the
      biggest 10-year savings.
   3. Options: optimizer builds the top 2 or 3 parts lists inside budget, goal
      and roof size.
   4. Simulation: hourly, with PVGIS-ERA5 weather.
   5. ROI table: grid only, solar, solar with battery over 10 years.
   6. Layout, Passport, rewards.
   7. Ask the rules: net metering, tariffs, DOE circular, with source links.

Q4.2  What is a Solar Passport?
A: Proof of going solar. Sol issues it with your plan and signs it (Ed25519).
   It has a shareable page and a QR code. Anyone can verify it at /verify/<id>.
   It does NOT prove your real roof or usage; those are inputs. [BUILT]

Q4.3  What are the three Passport stages?
A: Plan, installed, redeemed. The installed fact is inside the signed record.

Q4.4  What can't it do?
A: Electrical design, shading analysis, multi-plane roofs, and permits. A
   licensed installer must confirm every result. Say this early and
   confidently. It is the correct scope. [NOT BUILT]

Q4.5  Does it work without an AI key?
A: Yes. The rule-based ScriptedProvider handles intake, tools and replies.
   /api/health shows the mode (gemini or scripted). [BUILT]

Q4.6  What languages does it support?
A: The agent has a language setting and NLU for language detection. We did not
   formally test every language; do not promise Tagalog quality. If asked,
   say: "It detects and replies in the user's language where the model
   supports it. We tested mainly English." [PARTIAL]

Q4.7  Can I upload a bill photo?
A: Yes. Bill photo reading works in chat (Gemini image input). In scripted
   mode there is no vision. [BUILT]

Q4.8  What are the starter pills?
A: Map my site, Recommend Installers, Compare packages, Brief on Solar. They
   use the same API as typed chat.

Q4.9  Can I save and revisit chats?
A: Yes: recents list, new chat, delete, export. Sign up, login and logout are
   built. [BUILT]

Q4.10 What is the "Overview" page?
A: Package comparison plus a math drawer that shows the sources behind each
   figure. [BUILT]

SECTION 5. NUMBERS, ACCURACY AND TRUST
==============================================================================

Q5.1  What stops the AI from making up numbers?
A: Three layers. (1) The system prompt and tool design: the model has no
   calculator, only tools. (2) Every tool returns structured results. (3) The
   number guard (guard.py) compares numbers in the reply against tool output
   and blocks or corrects mismatches. [BUILT]
   If pushed: no guard is perfect. It catches invented numbers; it cannot prove
   that a tool's input assumption is right. That is why assumptions are listed
   and flagged.

Q5.2  So can I fully trust the output?
A: No, and we say it in the product. Every result is an estimate for a
   licensed installer to confirm. Demo households are synthetic.

Q5.3  Where do weather numbers come from?
A: PVGIS-ERA5, a modeled dataset (not measured at your roof). It is bundled for
   Metro Manila at 12 and 20 degree tilt facing south. Other locations can be
   fetched live (up to 35 seconds) and otherwise fall back to a labelled
   approximation. [BUILT, with the approximation flagged]

Q5.4  Where do prices come from?
A: Seed catalog and 25 real installer packages from 6 installers with
   published prices and links, re-checkable via scripts/build_packages.py. The
   scraper code exists, but no retailer source is enabled (we did not clear
   robots.txt and terms for any). Cached or sample items are labeled.
   [PARTIAL]

Q5.5  Are the sample packages fake?
A: Some are, and they are always labeled "Sample" and shown only where no real
   package fits. [BUILT]

Q5.6  Which assumptions are unverified?
A: ASSUMPTIONS.yaml marks entries with verified: false. Examples: the Meralco
   generation charge used for export credit (a PLACEHOLDER to replace with the
   latest published figure), the tariff growth rates (0%, 3%, 5% per year,
   judgement, disclosed), and the modelling simplification that folds fixed
   charges into the per-kWh rate. The agent tells the user when these drive a
   result. Admit this plainly; it is a strength to be this open.

Q5.7  Why 3% tariff growth?
A: It is a disclosed judgement, not a sourced forecast. We show 0%
   (pessimistic) and 5% (optimistic) around it. It would be justified with the
   historical Meralco rate series; that is an open item.

Q5.8  How do you handle net metering credit?
A: Exports earn a credit at Meralco's blended generation cost, not the retail
   rate. The charge is editable per request, and a lower-credit case is shown.
   The current default value is a placeholder until replaced with the latest
   published charge.

Q5.9  Why 10 years?
A: One horizon for every figure makes comparison readable. Paybacks longer
   than 10 years display as "not within 10 years".

Q5.10 Does the battery pay for itself?
A: Not within 10 years in our example. It adds roughly ₱89k to ₱155k and saves
   roughly ₱50k to ₱110k of bills. It buys backup. The 80% self-use share with
   a battery is our assumption and it decides the result. State that
   dependency yourself before a judge finds it.

Q5.11 Where do the deck's comparison numbers come from?
A: A small illustrative model for one 450 kWh household (bigger than Meralco's
   typical 200 kWh household), the published ₱45 to ₱75 per watt, the Meralco
   October rate and PVGIS weather years. Flat tariff, no upkeep. The app runs
   the real hourly simulation. Do not present the deck table as app output.

Q5.12 How accurate is the roof measurement?
A: An OpenStreetMap outline or a hand-drawn polygon, with a stated outline
   confidence. Obstructions, shading, multiple roof planes and structural
   fitness are flagged for the installer, not solved. Judge a "centimeter
   accurate" claim as false; do not make it.

Q5.13 How was it tested?
A: About 23 backend test files run offline on a temporary SQLite database,
   covering agent flow, auth, guard and tools, engine, geo, irradiance, load,
   packages, passport, RAG, rewards, roof, routes, simulation and optimizer,
   and upload check. Live checks need RUN_LIVE=1. Do not quote a pass count
   unless you ran the suite that day.

Q5.14 What is the AI model, and is it reliable?
A: Gemini with function calling, with a fallback model, then the scripted
   agent. Default model names are marked [VERIFY] in config and are checked
   with scripts/verify_gemini.py. /api/health reports the mode so we know which
   one is live.

Q5.15 What happens when Gemini is overloaded or the internet fails?
A: The provider falls back to a second model, then to the scripted agent. The
   forms and buttons use the same API as chat. Prices and weather fall back to
   cached files. The demo does not die. [BUILT]

SECTION 6. PASSPORT, REWARDS AND TRUST LOOP
==============================================================================

Q6.1  What are the vouchers?
A: Signed codes tied to one Passport, issued after install confirmation. Sample
   examples: a free site survey, an installer discount on the recommended
   package. Values are samples until a partner funds them. [BUILT as sample;
   PITCH for funding]

Q6.2  Can someone fake or reuse one?
A: The code derives from Sol's signature, so it cannot be invented. It is only
   issued after the install is confirmed, and it redeems once (a repeat
   returns 409). [BUILT]

Q6.3  Who confirms the install?
A: Honest answer: there is no installer confirm flow yet. In the demo only the
   seeded owner (dela.cruz@sol.test) has an installed Passport, with a sample
   installer flagged sample: true inside the signed record. Say this clearly.
   [PARTIAL]

Q6.4  Who pays for rewards?
A: Today, nobody, because values are samples. The pitch: installers or
   retailers fund them because they receive a customer who arrives with a
   layout and a checked price. This is a pitch, not an agreement. [PITCH]

Q6.5  Is this a Meralco reward?
A: No. Meralco is not involved and Sol promises no Meralco benefit. Vouchers
   have no cash value.

Q6.6  Why not points or tiers?
A: They need a program owner and rules we cannot invent. A voucher works with
   one Passport and needs no account.

Q6.7  How would usage rewards work?
A: A monthly generation reading from a REC meter would be added to the
   Passport and unlock a usage reward. The demo has no meter data, so that
   reward shows as locked. [NOT BUILT]

Q6.8  Isn't the Passport easy to forge or self-issue?
A: It cannot be forged without the private signing key. But a user can issue a
   plan Passport for any inputs, and we say a Passport does not prove the real
   roof or usage. Value comes from the installer-confirmed stage.
   If pushed: key loss or leak is the main risk. The key
   (backend/data/passport_ed25519.key) is gitignored; losing it means issued
   Passports can no longer be verified. Back it up. Rotation is not built.

Q6.9  How does verification work?
A: GET /verify/<id> renders an HTML page and the API checks the Ed25519
   signature against the stored record. There is also a paste-to-verify box on
   the Saved Records page. [BUILT]

Q6.10 Does a Passport help the user beyond rewards?
A: It is a portable record to hand an installer as a quote brief, and a
   reference when speaking to Meralco about the grid pathway.

==============================================================================
SECTION 9. TECHNICAL DEEP DIVE
==============================================================================

Q9.1  What is the stack?
A: Backend: Python 3.12, FastAPI. Frontend: Vite, React, TypeScript. Storage:
   Supabase Postgres or SQLite (auto fallback). Simulation: pvlib with
   PVGIS-ERA5. AI: Gemini function calling plus a rule-based provider.
   Retrieval: BM25 keyword search over a local document library. Signing:
   Ed25519. Auth: scrypt password hashes and hashed opaque tokens (30 day
   expiry). Streaming: Server-Sent Events.

Q9.2  Architecture in one sentence?
A: A FastAPI service where a runner orchestrates one agent turn (lock, provider
   with fallback, tools, number guard, reply), over deterministic domain
   services and a database, serving a React frontend.

Q9.3  What was hardest technically?
A: Hourly battery dispatch across many weather years, an optimizer constrained
   by budget and roof size, reliable price fetching, and a signed Passport with
   install confirmation. Also making a language model refuse to invent numbers.

Q9.4  Why RAG, and why BM25 not embeddings?
A: RAG gives the agent Meralco and DOE rules with source links so it does not
   answer rule questions from memory. BM25 keyword search works offline, is
   deterministic, needs no embedding service, and is good enough for a small
   curated corpus. RAG supplies facts only; numbers come from tools.

Q9.5  What if the document search gets it wrong?
A: Every answer shows its source passage and link so the user can check, and
   when nothing is found the agent says so.

Q9.6  How does the optimizer work?
A: It filters the catalog by roof fit, budget and system type (grid-tied,
   hybrid, off-grid), then ranks by the goal. The model can only pick from the
   catalog. [BUILT]

Q9.7  How do you estimate household load?
A: From kWh or bill using three templates (a simplified profile). It is an
   estimate, which is why real meter data is a listed next step.

Q9.8  How do you handle concurrency and abuse?
A: A per-session lock allows one turn at a time, a sliding-window rate limiter
   guards the Gemini quota, there are upload size and file count limits, and a
   75 second turn timeout. [BUILT]
   Honest gap: several engine, roof and geocode endpoints may be open without
   auth and call external services (Nominatim, Overpass, PVGIS). They need
   auth or rate limits before public release. Only claim what you verified.

Q9.9  Is the LLM prompt-injectable?
A: Uploaded files and web text are untrusted. The tool-only-numbers design
   limits the damage, because injected text cannot make the model produce an
   unchecked figure. We did not run a formal red-team; do not say it is
   immune.

Q9.10 How do you scrape prices responsibly?
A: The scraper checks robots.txt and has no retailer enabled until its terms
   are read. Packages come from published installer pages and the build script
   re-checks them.

Q9.11 What if a third-party service is down (Nominatim, Overpass, PVGIS)?
A: Address search, roof outlines and weather fall back to bundled or cached
   data, and the roof can always be drawn by hand. Nominatim returns 403 for a
   placeholder CONTACT_EMAIL, so a real address must be set. Check
   /api/health before the demo.

Q9.12 Why pvlib?
A: It is the standard open-source library for PV modelling, peer reviewed and
   widely used, so we did not write our own physics.

Q9.13 What does "hourly simulation over 16 weather years" mean?
A: We run the system against 16 years of hourly modeled weather rather than a
   single "typical" year. That is how we produce a bad year, an expected case
   and a best case.

Q9.14 Where is the data stored and is it safe?
A: The demo uses synthetic households. Passwords use scrypt, tokens are stored
   hashed. Uploads are processed per session and not stored. No real customer
   data is used in the demo. A production deployment would need a privacy
   review (the Philippine Data Privacy Act) that we have not done.

Q9.15 What is in the repo?
A: backend/ (FastAPI app, tests, migrations, data, scripts), frontend/
   (Vite/React/TS), deck/ (3D voxel pitch deck), docs/ (plan, pitch script,
   Q&A guides, status checklist), scripts/ (dev launchers).

Q9.16 How would it scale?
A: The API is stateless apart from the database and a per-process lock and
   rate limiter. Scaling out needs those moved to a shared store. A single
   process is fine for the demo.

SECTION 10. AI SAFETY, ETHICS AND PRIVACY
==============================================================================

Q10.1 Is the advice a financial or engineering guarantee?
A: No. It is a decision aid. Every result is an estimate for a licensed
   installer to confirm. This is stated in the product and Passport.

Q10.2 Could Sol push people into a bad purchase?
A: It is designed to do the opposite: show the battery not paying back, show a
   bad year, flag unverified assumptions, and list questions to ask the
   installer. We have not run a user study.

Q10.3 Does it favor certain installers?
A: Registry results are shown as "registered, not endorsed". Packages come
   from six installers because those were the ones with checkable published
   prices, not because of any commercial tie. No one paid for placement.

Q10.4 What personal data does it handle?
A: Account email and password hash, the address and bill figures the user
   types, and optional uploaded images. The demo is synthetic. Production
   would need consent flows, retention limits and a privacy review.

Q10.5 What about hallucinated rules (e.g. wrong Net Metering fees)?
A: Rule answers come from retrieved passages with source links, not model
   memory. If the library lacks it, the agent says it cannot find it.

Q10.6 Is the dataset licensed for use?
A: PVGIS and OpenStreetMap are open data with attribution requirements.
   Documents are summarized in our own words with source links.
   Confirm attribution is visible in the UI before submission.

Q10.7 Did you use AI to write the code?

   planning and data collection before. Agree on this answer before the pitch.
   If AI tools were used, say so; do not deny it.

==============================================================================
SECTION 12. TOUGH, HOSTILE AND TRICK QUESTIONS
==============================================================================

Q12.1 "This is just a chatbot with a calculator."
A: The chatbot is the front door. Behind it are a satellite-based roof outline,
   a weather-driven hourly simulation, an optimizer, a rules library, a guard
   and a signing service. Take the chat away and the forms still work through
   the same API.

Q12.2 "Your numbers are fake, aren't they?"
A: The households are synthetic by design and we say so. The method is real:
   PVGIS weather, pvlib physics, published prices. Unverified assumptions are
   listed and flagged, and we can show the register.

Q12.3 "No installer has signed up. Where is the traction?"
A: There is none, and we won't pretend. This is a hackathon prototype. Rewards
   funding and install confirmation are proposals, labeled as such.

Q12.4 "Why would an installer pay for this?"
A: A hypothesis: pre-qualified customers who arrive with a layout and a budget
   convert faster. We have not tested it. The first step is a pilot with one
   registered installer.

Q12.5 "What if Meralco changes its rules or rates?"
A: Rates and rules live in data (ASSUMPTIONS.yaml and the document library),
   not in code, and the generation charge is editable per request. Updating
   means updating data and re-running the corpus builder. It does require
   upkeep.

Q12.6 "Isn't the Passport a gimmick?"
A: Fair challenge. It is only valuable if third parties honor it. Today it is
   a verifiable record and a quote brief. Its value depends on installers and
   partners funding rewards, which is a pitch. The signature mechanism is real
   and working.

Q12.7 "Why should we believe your simulation?"
A: It uses pvlib with modeled PVGIS-ERA5 hourly weather over many years, which
   is a standard approach, and we show a bad year and best case. We did not
   validate against real meter data from a Philippine installation. That is
   the biggest accuracy gap, and a pilot would close it.

Q12.8 "What is the biggest weakness?"
A: Pick one, say it first: (1) assumptions marked unverified, (2) household
   load is estimated, not metered, (3) roof outline lacks shading and
   structure, (4) no real partner yet. All four are disclosed in the product.

Q12.9 "What if the model gives a wrong answer despite the guard?"
A: The guard catches numbers not produced by tools. It cannot detect a wrong
   interpretation (for example, misreading a bill photo). That is why the app
   shows the inputs it used and lets the user edit them.

Q12.10 "Why not build this as a spreadsheet?"
A: A spreadsheet needs the user to know the inputs and rules. Sol finds the
   roof, asks plain questions, runs thousands of hourly calculations, and
   explains in the user's words, with sources.

Q12.11 "Is this legal advice or engineering sign-off?"
A: Neither. A licensed installer and the utility decide what is approved.

Q12.12 "What if the demo breaks?"
A: Hold a backup video. Also: the app runs without Gemini (scripted mode), and
   weather for Metro Manila is bundled locally. Check /api/health and
   CONTACT_EMAIL before going on stage.

Q12.13 "What did you cut, and why?"
A: Photo auto-outline, shading, 3D view and retailer scraping. We cut from the
   bottom of the tier list to keep the core loop reliable.

Q12.14 "Can I try to break it?"
A: Yes. Invite them to ask for a number the tools do not give. The guard
   should refuse. Do this only if you rehearsed it.
