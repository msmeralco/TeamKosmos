Sol note: Sol has no vetted or accredited installer list, so it does not name or rank installers as trusted. 

Sol note: the closest official source is the DOE Solar PV Installer Registry (see the separate registry note). It lists registered firms, but the DOE states it does not endorse them. Searches for "best solar installers in the Philippines" mostly return rankings that installers publish about themselves. Use the checks below and ask every installer for a written quote.

What Meralco says to look for in a provider: choose an experienced provider that follows electrical and interconnection standards, documents the proposed equipment completely, secures the applicable permits, completes the required corrections, supports testing and commissioning, and offers dependable after-sales support. Keep copies of the design, equipment data, permits, inspection records, warranties and final settings.

Questions to put to any installer: Which exact panels, inverter and battery are in the quote, and what are their warranties? Who files the Meralco net-metering application and the local permits, including the Certificate of Final Electrical Inspection? Who pays for corrections after inspection? What is covered after installation, and who answers a service call? Does the quote assume export (net metering) or zero export?

Meralco's warning on safety: an improperly installed or untested system can damage equipment, cause fires or electric shock, energize lines during an outage if anti-islanding is missing, and upset power quality for neighbors. Never operate in parallel with the grid or export before Meralco's review, metering, testing and commissioning are complete.

Cheapest is not always best: the installed price is only part of the cost over the life of the system. Compare equipment quality, warranties, who handles permits, and service support alongside the price.
