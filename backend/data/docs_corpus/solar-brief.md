### Solar for a Meralco home, in short

Panels make power while the sun is up. Your home uses that power first. What it does not use either goes to the Meralco grid for a bill credit, or is kept from going out, depending on the setup you choose. At night, or when the panels fall short, you draw from Meralco as usual.

**Three ways to connect**

| Path | Sends power to the grid? | Bill credits? | What you file |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net Metering | Yes | Yes, at a lower rate than you pay (bank for later bills, not cash) | Application to Meralco, local permit and inspection, agreement. Up to 100 kW |
| Zero-Export system | No | No | Notify Meralco if connected to the grid, plus a building permit. Households do not need an ERC Certificate of Compliance |
| Micro Solar System | Rarely | Only if you also enroll in Net Metering | Notify Meralco only. Up to 1 kW of panels with an 800 W micro-inverter. No building permit |

**Costs to expect with Net Metering:** no fee just to apply, and Meralco's technical study and testing are not charged to you. You may still pay your system and installer, local permits and inspection, an ERC fee of ₱1,500, and a meter cost difference of up to ₱3,000 for homes.

**Blackouts:** a grid-connected system switches itself off in an outage, to keep line workers safe. A battery can keep essentials running only if the system is designed for it.

**Four things to check before you invest** (Meralco's list):
- **Right size:** match the system to the power you use in the daytime, so it is neither too big nor too small.
- **Right location:** surface condition, strength, direction, tilt and shade.
- **Right provider:** a qualified installer with complete equipment details and dependable after-sales support. Sol has not vetted any installer.
- **The requirements:** the latest checklist and early contact with your city for permits and the Certificate of Final Electrical Inspection.

Everything Sol shows is an estimate for a licensed installer to confirm.
