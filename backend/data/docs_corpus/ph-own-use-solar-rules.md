Summary written for Sol from a Rappler report on DOE Department Circular DC2026-08-0017. It covers solar systems meant for the owner's own use, not for selling power back to the grid. Meralco's own FAQ (https://www.meralco.com.ph/residential/energy-solutions/solar-and-renewables/distributed-energy-resources) repeats the same categories and requirements.

The circular has three kinds of system: Micro-Solar Systems (MSS), connected Self-Generating Facility Zero-Export Solar Systems (SGF ZESS), and isolated SGF ZESS.

Micro-Solar System (MSS): a small plug-and-play setup. Panels total no more than 1 kW, paired with a micro-inverter of at most 0.8 kW, and the system must meet electrical and safety requirements. The owner only notifies the distribution utility, such as Meralco, using the DOE form. No building permit and no ERC Certificate of Compliance is required. With an analog meter, the owner does not have to wait for a replacement before installing: the utility has up to 12 months after notification to swap the meter, and the old one can be used meanwhile.

Connected SGF ZESS: stays connected to the grid but uses equipment that stops excess solar power from flowing into it. There is no capacity ceiling. The owner must notify the utility and get a building permit. A new permit may not be needed if the local government confirms an existing building permit is enough.

Isolated SGF ZESS: runs completely apart from the grid. No notification to the utility is needed, but a building permit is still required.

ERC Certificate of Compliance for SGF ZESS: households, clinics, hospitals and other medical facilities are exempt. Other users, such as businesses, generally still need one.

Utilities such as Meralco can no longer require pre-installation clearances, technical permits or inspection fees for these systems. They may still study the effect on their network, but at their own cost, and they cannot use the study to delay or block the installation.

No credits for exports: any electricity sent back to the grid under these arrangements earns no bill credits or payments. To earn credits for excess power, the owner must enroll in net metering and sign a net-metering agreement with the utility, which has its own paperwork.

Background from the same report: the Philippines gets about 25% of its energy from renewables, with national targets of 35% of the generation mix by 2030 and 50% by 2040.
