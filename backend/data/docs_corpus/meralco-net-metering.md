How it works

Generate and use renewable energy

Your system supplies the property first while it is generating.

Export eligible excess energy

Surplus electricity can flow through the approved connection to the grid.

Import energy when needed

The Meralco grid supplies the property when renewable generation is not enough.

Receive bill credits

Eligible exports earn peso credits under the applicable Net Metering rules.

What is Net Metering?

Net Metering is an arrangement under the Renewable Energy Act that lets a qualified customer operate an on-site renewable-energy system in parallel with the distribution grid. The property uses the electricity it produces first. Eligible excess electricity is recorded when it is exported to the grid and earns a peso credit on the customer’s bill.

A bi-directional meter records imports from Meralco and exports from the approved system separately. Imported electricity and exported electricity use different rate components, so an exported kilowatt-hour is not exchanged one-for-one at the full retail rate.

The program at a glance

Enabled by law

Republic Act No. 9513 established the Net Metering pathway for qualified on-site renewable-energy systems.

Eligible capacity

The program ceiling is not more than 100 kW per qualified small generation entity. Final approval depends on the proposed system and local network conditions.

Energy export credits

Eligible excess electricity exported through the approved setup earns peso credits under the applicable monthly export-credit rate.

Use your own renewable energy first

Electricity generated on-site supplies the appliances and equipment running at the property before electricity is drawn from the grid.

Export eligible excess energy

When generation is higher than current use, eligible surplus electricity flows through the bi-directional meter to the Meralco grid.

Import energy when generation is low

When renewable generation cannot meet demand, electricity flows from the Meralco grid through the same bi-directional meter to the property.

Why registration matters

Safety

Technical review, inspection, approved metering, testing, and commissioning help the system operate safely with the grid.

Grid stability

Approved protection and a technical assessment help prevent unacceptable power-quality or reliability effects.

Sustainability

Using renewable electricity on-site can reduce dependence on grid-supplied energy while the system is generating.

Potential bill savings

On-site use reduces imported electricity, while eligible exports may earn credits. Results vary with use, weather, design, rates, and maintenance.

Supporting your move to solar and renewables

Renewable energy can supply homes and businesses with cleaner on-site electricity. Net Metering adds a safe, approved way for qualified customers to export eligible excess generation and receive bill credits.

Review the application process

Electricity produced by the renewable-energy system supplies the property first. When generation is higher than demand, eligible excess energy can be exported. When generation is lower, the property continues to import electricity from Meralco.

See imports and exports separately

A bi-directional meter records electricity delivered by Meralco and eligible electricity exported by the approved system as two separate energy flows. A separate REC meter may be used to measure gross renewable generation when selected.

The eligible exported kilowatt-hours are multiplied by the applicable export rate to calculate the peso credit for the period.

The Net Metering peso credit is applied to eligible bill charges under the current rules, and any available balance is handled through the approved credit process.

Why safety and compliance cannot wait

Equipment damage

An improperly installed or untested system can cause faults that damage renewable-energy equipment, appliances, or connected electrical facilities.

Fire and electric shock

Non-compliant wiring, protection, or installation work can create electrical fire and electrocution hazards.

Lineworker safety

A system without correct anti-islanding and isolation protection can energize lines during an outage or maintenance activity.

Power quality and grid stability

An incorrect interconnection can create voltage, power-quality, or service disturbances that affect the property and nearby customers.

Getting started and eligibility

Who can apply for Net Metering?

Qualified new and existing Meralco customers may apply, including residential, business, industrial, and government customers. The account, proposed system, service arrangement, technical design, and local network conditions must meet the applicable requirements.

Is Net Metering exclusively for solar PV technology? What other technologies can I use?

No. Solar PV is the most common option, but qualified on-site renewable-energy systems may also use wind, hydro, biomass, or another technology allowed by the program. Every proposal remains subject to Meralco’s technical and interconnection review.

Does the Net Metering program allow me to save on my electric bill?

It can. Using your own renewable electricity reduces the energy you import while the system is generating, and eligible exports may earn bill credits. Actual results depend on electricity use, weather, equipment, system design, maintenance, rates, and program rules.

Download and complete the current application form, review the documentary checklist, and submit the required account, applicant, project, equipment, and representative information through an approved Meralco channel. Meralco then confirms eligibility and begins the technical review.

What are the Net Metering application requirements?

Requirements cover the Meralco account and applicant, proposed system and provider, panels, inverters, optional batteries and REC meter, authorization when a representative applies, technical study information, local permits and CFEI, the Net Metering Agreement, applicable payments, and any required affidavit or waiver. How long does the Net Metering application process take?

Timing depends on a complete application, permits, technical-study results, customer corrections, network conditions, inspection readiness, agreements, payments, metering, and commissioning. Meralco will provide the next action and status at each stage; the full journey does not have one unconditional completion date.

Technical checks

What is a Distribution Impact Study and why is it required?

A Distribution Impact Study assesses whether the local distribution system can safely accommodate the proposed interconnection. It considers factors such as system capacity, transformer loading, voltage, power quality, protection, and available hosting capacity, and identifies whether further study or network work is needed.

Meters, billing, and credits

What is a bi-directional meter and why do I need it?

A bi-directional meter records electricity moving in both directions. Import registers measure electricity delivered from the Meralco grid to the property, while export registers measure eligible surplus electricity delivered from the approved system to the grid. These separate readings are used for Net Metering billing.

What is a REC meter and do I need one under the 2025 amended Net Metering rules?

A Renewable Energy Certificate meter measures the gross electricity generated by the renewable-energy system. It is separate from the required bi-directional import/export meter and is voluntary under the amended rules. If you decline it, the current process requires the applicable affidavit and waiver and uses the approved method to estimate generation.

When can I start seeing Net Metering bill credits?

Credits begin after the approved meters are installed or configured, the renewable-energy system passes testing and commissioning, and the special meter reading establishes the start of Net Metering service. Eligible exports are then reflected through the succeeding billing process.

Why is the export energy rate lower than the import energy rate?

Imported electricity includes the retail-rate components that apply to the service. Eligible exported electricity earns a peso credit using the export-credit method required by current rules, based on the applicable monthly blended generation cost rather than the full retail rate. The export value can therefore change from month to month.

Do Net Metering bill credits expire?

Unused Net Metering credits are banked and carried forward for application to current or future bills while the account and agreement remain eligible. They are bill credits rather than a cash payment, and their treatment at closure or transfer follows the current rules and account conditions.

Can bill credits be transferred if I move locations?

Credits do not simply move to a different property. A transfer may be allowed for certain property-ownership changes when the required account, Net Metering Agreement, and Certificate of Compliance conditions are met. Contact Meralco before changing ownership, closing the account, or moving.

Customer responsibilities and compliance

Will Meralco need access to my meter or premises for inspection or maintenance?

Yes. Meralco may need safe, reasonable access to inspect the service entrance and renewable-energy facility, install or maintain approved meters, perform testing and commissioning, verify compliance, or investigate a metering or safety concern. Coordinate schedules and keep the required equipment accessible.

What happens if I modify or expand my solar PV system after approval?

Contact Meralco before changing panel or inverter capacity, equipment models, batteries, protection, transfer equipment, wiring, or operating mode. A material change may require updated forms, technical review, inspection, metering, agreement, or commissioning before it can operate in parallel with the grid.

Costs and fees

How much does it cost to apply for Net Metering?

There is no fee simply to submit the application, and the Distribution Impact Study, testing, and commissioning are not charged to the applicant under current program rules. Other project, permit, meter, ERC, correction, study, or network costs may still apply depending on the installation.

What other fees should I expect?

Possible costs include the renewable-energy system and provider, LGU permits and inspection, customer-side corrections, the ERC Certificate of Compliance application fee, an applicable Difference in Meter Cost, and a case-specific Distribution Asset Study or network requirement. Meralco or the responsible authority will provide the applicable assessment before payment.

Safety and protection

Can I use a Battery Energy Storage System for my renewable-energy installation?

Yes, a proposed system may include a battery, but it is not automatically approved. The application must identify the battery and transfer-switch details, and Meralco and the provider must confirm the protection, isolation, metering, operating mode, and technical review that apply to the design.

How can I ensure my installer follows standards?

Choose a reputable, experienced provider that documents the proposed equipment, follows electrical and interconnection standards, secures the applicable permits, completes required corrections, and supports testing and commissioning. Keep copies of the design, equipment data, permits, inspection records, warranties, and final settings.

What are the risks of installing solar without registering under Net Metering?

An unreviewed grid connection can create equipment damage, fire, electric-shock, lineworker, power-quality, and grid-stability risks. A one-way meter may also record unintended exports as additional consumption. Do not operate in parallel or export until the required review, protection, metering, testing, and commissioning are complete.

Other questions

What happens if my solar system exports power unintentionally while I am not enrolled in Net Metering?

Stop unapproved export and contact the provider and Meralco. An ordinary one-way meter may record reverse energy as additional consumption, and an unapproved interconnection may create safety and power-quality risks. Meralco will identify the correct registration, Zero Export, correction, or interconnection path for the installation.

What if I install solar panels without enrolling in the Net-Metering program? I want to use all the electricity my panels generate and do not want to export to the grid.

For a customer-owned system that generates electricity for your own use without exporting, review the SGF Zero Export Solar System or Micro Solar System guidance. These programs have their own system, safety and notification requirements.

What happens during a power outage—will my renewable-energy system still work?

Do not assume it will. A grid-connected system must stop energizing Meralco lines during an outage. Whether the property has approved backup power depends on the inverter, battery, transfer equipment, isolation design, and operating arrangement. Never bypass anti-islanding or isolation protection.

Can I combine Net Metering with other Meralco programs?

Depending on the customer class and service, you may also be eligible for energy-efficiency, Peak/Off-Peak, or other Meralco programs. Each program has its own metering, billing, technical, and enrollment rules, so ask Meralco to assess how the options work together for the account.
